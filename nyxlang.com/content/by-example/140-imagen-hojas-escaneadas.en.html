An ERP receives scanned sheets (1275 x 1650 at 150 dpi, 2550 x 3300 at 300 dpi) and makes a lightweight version of each one on upload. A sheet is NOT a 12-megapixel photo: the thumbnail wanted is 1600 px on a side, almost the source size. That changes what to expect from std/image. The sample sheet is built inside the recipe — white paper with lines of text — so it needs no file.

hoja: jpeg 1275x1650, 199862 bytes guardar: jpeg 1236x1600, 208944 bytes destino 1600: decodifica a 1275x1650 (reducción 1/1) destino 300: decodifica a 638x825 (reducción 1/2) salida: jpeg 1236x1600, 208944 bytes salida: jpeg 232x300, 23637 bytes

The reduced decoding (1/2, 1/4, 1/8) that photos use does not apply here: the rule is to reduce while at least twice the target's pixels remain, and 1650 to 1600 leaves no room to reduce anything. The whole sheet is decoded, shrunk a little (Lanczos3, byte for byte equal to Pillow) and encoded again, so the work is proportional to the sheet, not to the target. For a small preview — say 300 px — there is reduction: the sheet decodes at 1/2, as the output shows.

That is why the build mode matters. nyx build without --release does not optimize (-O0), and nyx build --release passes -O2. Measured on the 1275 x 1650 sheet (aarch64, CPU), image_thumbnail(sheet, 1600, 1600, 80) takes about 1.0 s at -O0 and about 0.25 s at -O2; the 2550 x 3300 sheet costs about four times that. Before the image-speed work the same call took 3.6 s at -O0.
