An application that will email invoices wants to check, by itself, that the sender's domain has its DNS records in order — and to show the administrator what is missing instead of letting messages land in spam. SPF is a TXT record of the domain starting with v=spf1; DKIM is the CNAME (or TXT) of <selector>._domainkey.<domain>; DMARC is a TXT record of _dmarc.<domain> starting with v=DMARC1.

== dominio completo SPF: ok (v=spf1 include:_spf.proveedor.com -all) DKIM: ok (apunta a sel1.dkim.proveedor.com) DMARC: ok (p=reject) == dominio a medias SPF: ok (v=spf1 -all) DKIM: falta el registro del selector DMARC: presente pero p=none (solo monitorea, no protege) == sin nada SPF: falta el registro TXT 'v=spf1 ...' del dominio DKIM: falta el registro del selector DMARC: falta el registro TXT de _dmarc

dns_txt returns ONE element per record (the 255-byte strings of a long record already come joined), so looking for the prefix is enough. dns_is_nxdomain and dns_is_nodata tell the name does not exist from it exists but published no such record, without reading messages. The checks themselves are pure functions over the records, kept apart from the network query, which is why the demo can run three domains — complete, half-configured and empty — without any DNS.

The module gives the records, not the verdict: evaluating an SPF include: or DKIM alignment is the caller's job. With no arguments, nyx run runs the demonstration; nyx run -- midominio.com sel1 queries the system's real DNS. Without a nameserver in /etc/resolv.conf (or on a system without that file), call dns_set_server("ip") first.
