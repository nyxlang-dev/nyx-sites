Some banks and payment providers require the merchant to identify itself with its own certificate on top of an API key: the server asks for the certificate during the handshake and cuts the connection if none it accepts arrives. HttpOpts presents it PER REQUEST with http_opts_client_cert(opts, cert_pem, key_pem).

rechazado antes de conectar: invalid 22

Certificate and private key are PEM CONTENT, not paths — bring them with read_file or include_bytes. The certificate goes first, with any intermediates after it in the same text, and the key cannot have a passphrase. Everything is validated BEFORE connecting: one without the other, an unreadable PEM, or a key that does not match the certificate give Err invalid 22 without opening the socket. That is what the recipe shows, even against a closed port.

It only applies to that request; the rest of the process presents nothing. When the bank uses a private CA it combines with cas_extra (recipe 122): o.cas_extra = read_file("certs/ca-banco.pem"). The recipe needs no network, so the output is the early rejection and nothing more.
